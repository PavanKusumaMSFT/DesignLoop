# User Personas — HITS Study Participation Experience

> **Source study:** [HITS Study 6048410](https://hits.microsoft.com/study/6048410)

These personas represent three distinct user archetypes observed during the Discover phase. They are grounded in research pain points around study discoverability, task completion friction, participant-study matching, and feedback loops.

---

## Persona 1: Alex Chen — The Time-Constrained Contributor

![Persona Badge: Participant](https://via.placeholder.com/80x80?text=AC)

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Name** | Alex Chen |
| **Role** | Senior Product Manager, Microsoft Teams |
| **Location** | Redmond, WA |
| **Tenure** | 6 years at Microsoft |
| **Age** | 34 |
| **HITS usage** | 2–3 studies per quarter |
| **Tech proficiency** | High — power user of internal tools, comfortable with Azure DevOps, Figma reviews, and telemetry dashboards |
| **Devices** | Surface Laptop Studio (primary), iPhone 15 Pro (secondary) |

### Goals

1. **Quick in, quick out** — Complete studies in 10 minutes or less during gaps between meetings.
2. **Relevant studies only** — Wants to see studies related to Teams, collaboration tools, or PM workflows — not studies about hardware she's never used.
3. **Feel the impact** — Wants to know that her 10 minutes led to something tangible; otherwise, she'll stop participating.

### Frustrations

- 😤 "I opened HITS last week and the first 15 studies were about Xbox accessories. I closed the tab."
- 😤 Started a study on mobile between meetings, got halfway through, and the session timed out — all progress lost.
- 😤 Never received any follow-up on the 8 studies she completed last year. Doesn't know if her feedback mattered.
- 😤 Screener questions sometimes disqualify her after she's already invested 3 minutes answering them.

### Quote

> *"I want to help make our products better — I literally do this for a living. But HITS makes it feel like a chore instead of a contribution. If I could get a push notification for a relevant 5-minute study, I'd do one every week."*

### Key Scenarios

| Scenario | Current Experience | Desired Experience |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Finding a relevant study | Scrolls through 30+ listings, gives up after 5 min | Opens HITS to a personalized "For You" feed; top study is Teams-related and takes 8 min |
| Completing a study on mobile | Starts on iPhone, session times out, progress lost | Saves progress automatically; resumes on laptop later |
| Post-study follow-up | Generic "Thank you" page, no further contact | Receives a 2-sentence summary 2 weeks later: "Your feedback helped us redesign the meeting join flow" |

### Relationship to HMW Statements

- **Primary:** HMW 1 (Discoverability), HMW 2 (Task Completion Friction)
- **Secondary:** HMW 4 (Feedback Loops)

## Persona 2: Priya Sharma — The Insight-Driven Researcher

![Persona Badge: Researcher](https://via.placeholder.com/80x80?text=PS)

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Name** | Priya Sharma |
| **Role** | Senior UX Researcher, Microsoft 365 Design |
| **Location** | Hyderabad, India |
| **Tenure** | 4 years at Microsoft |
| **Age** | 29 |
| **HITS usage** | Creates 3–5 studies per month; also participates in 1–2 studies from other teams |
| **Tech proficiency** | High — expert in UserTesting, Qualtrics, and HITS authoring tools; comfortable with R/Python for data analysis |
| **Devices** | Surface Pro 9 (primary), Pixel 8 (secondary) |

### Goals

1. **Recruit the right participants, fast** — Needs 15 participants with specific profiles (e.g., "PMs who use Loop daily on desktop") filled within 3 business days.
2. **Minimize noise in data** — Wants every response to come from someone who genuinely matches the study criteria; disqualified responses waste analysis time.
3. **Close the loop** — Wants to share key findings back with participants to build goodwill and encourage future participation.
4. **Streamline study creation** — Reduce the overhead of setting up eligibility criteria, screeners, and task flows.

### Frustrations

- 😤 Recruitment takes 7–10 days on average; sometimes she resorts to hallway recruiting or Slack posts to fill slots.
- 😤 ~20% of completed responses come from participants who don't match the target profile, requiring manual data cleaning.
- 😤 No built-in way to send a "here's what we learned" summary to past participants.
- 😤 The study authoring interface lacks templates, forcing her to rebuild similar screener logic for every study.
- 😤 Can't see which participants have relevant experience before they self-enroll.

### Quote

> *"I spend more time recruiting and cleaning data than I do actually analyzing insights. HITS should be my research accelerator, not my bottleneck."*

### Key Scenarios

| Scenario | Current Experience | Desired Experience |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Recruiting for a targeted study | Sets broad filters, waits 7+ days, manually disqualifies 20% of enrollees | Defines target profile; HITS recommends and invites matched employees; fills in 2 days |
| Reviewing participant quality | Discovers mismatched participants only after analyzing responses | Pre-enrollment screener auto-qualifies participants; fit score visible before study launch |
| Sharing findings | Copies findings into an email, manually tracks who participated | One-click "Publish Impact Summary" in HITS; auto-sent to all study participants |
| Creating a new study | Builds from scratch each time; duplicates screener logic | Starts from a template library; reuses screener modules from past studies |

### Relationship to HMW Statements

- **Primary:** HMW 3 (Participant-Study Matching), HMW 4 (Feedback Loops)
- **Secondary:** HMW 1 (Discoverability — from the supply side)

## Persona 3: Jordan Blake — The Enthusiastic Newcomer

![Persona Badge: New Participant](https://via.placeholder.com/80x80?text=JB)

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Name** | Jordan Blake |
| **Role** | Software Engineer II, Azure DevOps |
| **Location** | Vancouver, BC, Canada |
| **Tenure** | 3 months at Microsoft |
| **Age** | 26 |
| **HITS usage** | Signed up 2 weeks ago, completed 1 study, abandoned 1 |
| **Tech proficiency** | High for coding tools (VS Code, GitHub); low familiarity with Microsoft's internal UX research ecosystem |
| **Devices** | Surface Laptop 5 (primary), Samsung Galaxy S24 (secondary) |

### Goals

1. **Make an impact early** — Wants to contribute to Microsoft's product culture beyond their immediate team.
2. **Understand the system** — Needs onboarding guidance: what is HITS, what happens with my data, how do I pick the right study?
3. **Build a participation habit** — Open to regular participation if the experience is smooth and rewarding.
4. **Learn about other products** — Sees studies as a way to explore parts of Microsoft they don't interact with daily.

### Frustrations

- 😤 The HITS landing page offered no orientation — just a wall of study cards with cryptic titles like "M365 Copilot Perf. Task B v3."
- 😤 Enrolled in a study meant for designers; completed it but felt their responses were probably useless.
- 😤 Tried to complete a study on mobile during lunch; the interface was clunky and buttons were cut off.
- 😤 Not sure if their manager supports spending work time on studies — no visible organizational endorsement.
- 😤 After completing their first study, received no follow-up. Unsure if they "did it right."

### Quote

> *"I heard about HITS in new-hire orientation and thought it sounded awesome. But when I opened it, I had no idea where to start. I just picked one at random and hoped for the best."*

### Key Scenarios

| Scenario | Current Experience | Desired Experience |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| First visit to HITS | Sees a dense catalog with no guidance; feels overwhelmed | Guided onboarding flow: "Welcome! Let's build your profile so we can recommend studies for you" |
| Choosing a study | Picks randomly; doesn't realize it's for a different role | Studies show clear eligibility badges ("Best for: Engineers", "Time: 10 min"); system gently warns if mismatch detected |
| Completing a study | Struggles with unresponsive mobile UI; unsure about question intent | Clean responsive interface; inline tooltips explain what each task is measuring |
| Post-study experience | Generic thank-you page; silence afterward | Confirmation email with estimated impact timeline; badge or point toward a participation milestone |

### Relationship to HMW Statements

- **Primary:** HMW 1 (Discoverability), HMW 3 (Participant-Study Matching)
- **Secondary:** HMW 2 (Task Completion Friction), HMW 4 (Feedback Loops)
